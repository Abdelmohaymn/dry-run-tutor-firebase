# Feature Specification: Firebase Hybrid AI Tutor

**Feature Branch**: `firebase-main`

**Created**: 2026-11-23

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description: "Firebase entry: new worker dry-run-tutor-firebase; app calls Gemini via Firebase AI Logic; worker keeps prompts, D1 state, leak filter, judge; offline Nano tutor. for the new hackathon"

## Context

Dry Run is entered in the Firebase #BuildHybridAI hackathon (Google, run on Devpost; submissions close 2026-11-30 2:00 PM PT; judging 2026-12-10 to 12-16). The entry is built on `firebase-main`, branched from the Nebius entry (`main`), without the PayPal Pro Plus subscription. The Nebius and PayPal entries, their repositories and their relays MUST stay untouched.

Hackathon rules that shape this spec:

- The app MUST call Firebase AI Logic at runtime, from shipped code, and MUST use App Check.
- On Android, the app MUST use the on-device model (Gemini Nano) through Firebase AI Logic hybrid inference.
- Judges score four equal criteria: how well it uses hybrid inference, whether it feels like a complete product, credible impact, and idea quality.
- Judges MUST be able to test the app free and without restriction. There is no paywall.

Today every model call goes app → our relay → Nebius (Nemotron). This feature moves the model call to the app (cloud Gemini, plus on-device Gemini Nano). The relay keeps everything else: prompts, ground truth, per-session state, the leak filter and the judge's post-processing.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-11-23

- Q: How does ground truth reach the cloud model, given Principle VI? → A: The relay sends the instructions per turn; the app holds them in memory only, never persists or bundles them. Principle VI is amended for this entry. Accepted because the problems are public samples and the users are the judges.
- Q: Do offline turns count toward the judged session? → A: No. They are not capped and not judge evidence. On reconnect the online tutor gets a short summary of them so the conversation continues.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Online session runs on cloud Gemini, unchanged for the learner (Priority: P1)

A judge installs the APK, opens today's problem and talks through an approach. The tutor pushes back with a counter-question, a failing case or the smallest useful hint, exactly as it does today. At the end the judge receives a verdict and the editorial. The difference is that every tutor and judge reply is now generated by cloud Gemini, called from the app through Firebase AI Logic. The relay still decides what the model is told, and checks what it says.

**Why this priority**: it is the entry. Without it the submission fails the hackathon's baseline check ("Firebase AI Logic imported and actually called"), and the product has no tutor.

**Independent Test**: on a phone with network, run one full session on any problem. The tutor holds the solution back, the verdict arrives, and the conversation shows that every reply came from cloud Gemini via Firebase AI Logic. No call goes to Nebius.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a learner online on today's problem, **When** they send a spoken turn, **Then** the tutor replies within the same time the learner is used to today, and the reply is produced by cloud Gemini through Firebase AI Logic.
2. **Given** a tutor reply that would reveal the solution, **When** the relay checks it, **Then** the learner never sees the leaking text, the same way as today.
3. **Given** a learner who ends the session, **When** the judge runs, **Then** the verdict is classified against the problem's human-authored ground truth, uses only that problem's approach keys, and is hedged ("likely passes").
4. **Given** the turn cap is reached, **When** the learner tries another turn, **Then** the relay refuses it as today, whatever the app asks for.

---

### User Story 2 - Offline, the on-device model keeps the learner talking (Priority: P2)

A learner on a train loses signal mid-session, or opens the app with no network. Instead of a dead "no connection" state, an on-device tutor (Gemini Nano) keeps the conversation going on the problem's public statement: it asks the learner to explain their approach, questions vague claims and asks for a small example. It never claims to know the answer and gives no verdict. The screen says plainly that the learner is talking to the offline tutor. When the network returns, the learner continues with the full online tutor.

**Why this priority**: this is the hybrid-inference story the judges score first, and the one the demo video leads with. It also removes the product's largest stated weakness ("there is no offline tutor", Principle VIII).

**Independent Test**: on a supported phone with Gemini Nano available, switch to airplane mode, open a problem and hold a 3-turn conversation. Replies come from the on-device model, the offline label is visible, and no verdict is offered. Switch the network back on and the next turn goes to the online tutor.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a supported phone with the on-device model ready and no network, **When** the learner sends a turn, **Then** the on-device tutor replies and the screen marks the reply as offline.
2. **Given** an offline conversation, **When** the learner asks for the answer, **Then** the offline tutor says it cannot give one and redirects to a question about their approach.
3. **Given** the network returns, **When** the learner sends the next turn, **Then** it goes to the online tutor, which receives a short summary of the offline turns so the conversation continues. The offline turns do not count against the turn cap and are not judge evidence; they stay visible in the transcript with their offline label.
4. **Given** a phone that does not support the on-device model, **When** it is offline, **Then** the learner sees today's behaviour (a retryable failed send), never a broken screen.

---

### User Story 3 - The app picks the right model by itself (Priority: P3)

The learner never chooses a model. The app uses cloud Gemini when online, and the on-device model when offline or when the cloud is unavailable (rate limit, outage). While the on-device model is still downloading on a supported phone, the app uses the cloud and says nothing about it.

**Why this priority**: it makes the hybrid feel like one product rather than two modes, which the "Design" criterion rewards. It also protects judges from shared free-tier quota errors during judging.

**Independent Test**: force the cloud to answer "rate limited" on a supported phone. The next turn is answered offline-style with the offline label, and the following turn goes back to the cloud once it is available.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the cloud refuses a turn with a rate-limit or capacity error, **When** the phone supports the on-device model, **Then** the turn is answered by the on-device tutor with the offline label, not shown as an error.
2. **Given** the on-device model is not yet downloaded, **When** the learner is online, **Then** the cloud is used and no download prompt blocks the conversation.

---

### User Story 4 - Judges can install, test and understand it (Priority: P3)

A hackathon judge downloads the APK from the repository's release page, follows a short README, and can tell in under five minutes what runs in the cloud, what runs on the phone, and how to see the offline tutor.

**Why this priority**: the submission is scored partly from the README and video. Judges may never run the app.

**Independent Test**: someone who has never seen the project follows only the README, installs the APK and reaches an offline reply.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the README, **When** a judge reads the "How it uses Firebase AI Logic" section, **Then** they can name where the cloud model and the on-device model are used.
2. **Given** a phone without on-device support, **When** a judge follows the README, **Then** the README tells them what they will and will not see.

### Edge Cases

- The on-device model is supported but still downloading: use the cloud online; offline, behave as an unsupported phone and say the offline tutor will be ready soon.
- The network drops between the app receiving the relay's prompt and calling the cloud: the turn falls back to the on-device tutor if available, otherwise it becomes a retryable failed send. The turn MUST NOT be counted twice against the cap.
- The cloud reply arrives but the relay cannot be reached to check it: the reply is not shown unchecked; the turn becomes retryable.
- The learner ends the session while offline: the streak is credited (Principle IV/VIII) and the judge runs on reconnect, as today.
- The shared free-tier quota runs out for everyone during judging: online turns fall back to the on-device tutor on supported phones, and others get a plain rate-limit message, never a crash.
- App Check fails (for example a sideloaded build without a valid token): the learner sees a clear "this build can't reach the tutor" message.
- The on-device model gives an off-topic or empty reply: it is replaced by a short fixed prompt asking the learner to continue explaining.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Cloud path**

- **FR-001**: Every online tutor reply and every judge verdict MUST be generated by cloud Gemini, called from the app through Firebase AI Logic. Nebius and Groq MUST NOT be used for tutor or judge text.
- **FR-002**: For each online turn the relay MUST supply what the model is told (instructions and problem context) and MUST check what the model returned before the learner sees it. The app MUST NOT build tutor or judge instructions itself.
- **FR-003**: The relay MUST keep enforcing the turn cap, session state, the hint ladder, the finish check and the per-turn judge evidence it records today, with the same outcomes as the current product.
- **FR-004**: The relay MUST keep the leak filter: a reply that reveals the solution MUST NOT reach the learner (Principle III).
- **FR-005**: The judge MUST keep classifying against human-authored ground truth with that problem's own approach keys, returning `none` when nothing matches (Principle II). The relay MUST validate and post-process the verdict as today.
- **FR-006**: The relay MUST send the model instructions (including ground truth) to the app per turn, single use. The app MUST hold them in memory only for that call: never written to disk, logs, crash reports or the local database, and never bundled in the APK. This knowingly relaxes Principle VI (see Clarifications).
- **FR-007**: The app MUST use App Check for every Firebase AI Logic call.

**On-device path**

- **FR-008**: On phones that support it, the app MUST use the on-device model through Firebase AI Logic hybrid inference when there is no network, and when the cloud refuses a turn for rate limit or capacity.
- **FR-009**: The offline tutor MUST work only from the problem's public half. It MUST NOT receive ground truth, hints or the editorial, and MUST NOT give a verdict.
- **FR-010**: Every offline reply MUST be labelled as coming from the offline tutor, in the conversation itself.
- **FR-011**: The offline tutor MUST follow the tutor's rules that do not need ground truth: one question at a time, never says "correct" or "wrong", asks for the approach, a small example or the cost of the approach.
- **FR-011a**: Offline turns MUST NOT count against the turn cap and MUST NOT feed the judge. When the learner is next online in the same session, the relay MUST receive a short summary of the offline turns (written from the learner's own words, at most a few sentences) and give it to the online tutor as context.
- **FR-012**: On phones without on-device support, offline behaviour MUST stay exactly as today (retryable failed send, Principle VIII).

**Relay and isolation**

- **FR-013**: The Firebase entry MUST use its own new relay, `dry-run-tutor-firebase`, with its own database. The Nebius relay (`dry-run-tutor`) and the PayPal relay MUST NOT be changed or redeployed.
- **FR-014**: Voice transcription (cloud speech and the platform fallback) MUST keep working as today, on the new relay.
- **FR-015**: Bring-your-own-key MUST NOT be needed for any feature in this entry. Screens that ask for a provider key MUST be hidden.
- **FR-016**: No Firebase analytics or other product-analytics collection may be enabled (Quality Gates: no third-party analytics SDK).

**Submission**

- **FR-017**: The repository MUST carry an open-source licence and a README section that explains, for judges, where the cloud model and on-device model are used, and how to see the offline tutor.
- **FR-018**: A release APK MUST be downloadable by judges, free, with no sign-in that judges cannot complete.

### Key Entities

- **Turn plan**: what the relay hands the app for one online turn — the model instructions and context, plus an identifier the relay uses to accept the reply. Single use.
- **Turn result**: the model's reply sent back to the relay for checking, and the checked text the learner sees.
- **Offline turn**: a turn answered on the device; carries the offline label; holds no ground truth.
- **Session state** (existing): turn count, hints revealed, judge evidence per turn, finish state — kept on the relay.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: 100% of online tutor and judge replies in a full session come from cloud Gemini through Firebase AI Logic; zero calls go to Nebius.
- **SC-002**: The existing tutor leak harness and judge harness pass at the same rate as on the Nemotron build, on every shipped problem.
- **SC-003**: On a supported phone in airplane mode, a learner gets an offline reply to 3 consecutive turns with no error screen.
- **SC-004**: An online turn's reply appears within 2 seconds of the current build's median, measured on the same phone and problem.
- **SC-005**: A first-time reader of the README can say where each model runs and install the APK in under 5 minutes.
- **SC-006**: The Nebius and PayPal relays receive zero deploys during this feature.

## Assumptions

- The hackathon judges are the only users. Cost and abuse are judged at that scale (as for the Nebius entry). A modified client that fabricates a model reply is accepted risk at that scale.
- The free Spark tier is enough for development and judging. The Google Account Quota form is not used.
- Firebase AI Logic for Flutter supports cloud Gemini only. The on-device path needs native Android code reached from Flutter. Native hybrid inference is experimental (beta).
- The on-device model is available only on recent AICore-enabled phones. The demo video is filmed on one. Most judges will see only the cloud path.
- The online turn needs two relay round-trips (get instructions, check reply) instead of one. Latency is checked by SC-004.
- Constitution Principles VI ("ground truth never leaves the server; the app talks to exactly one host") and VIII ("there is no offline tutor") conflict with this feature. They need an amendment before merge, recorded the same way the Nebius and PayPal amendments were.
- The public repository `dry-run-tutor-firebase` is created only at submission. Until then the work stays on local `firebase-main`.
